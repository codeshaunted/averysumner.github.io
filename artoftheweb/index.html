Week 2 - Ulises Carrión - Mail Art and the Big Monster from Second Thoughts
I appreciate how intentionally vague Carrion made the notion of the "big monster", as it allows us to easily retrofit it for our current situation with art on the web. In the time of mail art it may have been the postmaster, the post office clerks, the stamps, or all or none of the above as Carrion mentioned. But, in our day and age on the web, it could just as easily be our ISPs, web hosts, government censorship, etc.

Discussion Questions:
1. Carrion says "the best Mail Art pieces use the post as an integral, functional element of the work", what is an example of this concept for art on the web?
2. Carrion does not seem to believe mail art is democratic as it's a very small circle of artists. Has widespread access to viewing and publishing on the web made web art democratic in a way mail art never could have been?

Week 3 - Laurel Schwulst & Édouard U - Selections from Creative Independent and Arena How Do You Use the Internet Mindfully
I very much agree with what Schwulst is saying about how the web has become increasingly commercialized and strayed from its initial vision. The "network" of sites that people visit is becoming increasingly small, the vast majority of web traffic is isolated to google, facebook, etc. I feel like even as a child in the 2010s the web felt much wider and less concentrated than it does now to me. As for Edouard U's essay, I feel it connects to the same idea. Edouard says that they believe "conceptual isolation creates the death of meaning". I think with this increasingly concentrated web that meaning is dying. There is far less meaning in an Instagram post or a tweet than a personalized website.

Discussion Questions:
1. What's your personal metaphor for a website?
2. Does generative AI contribute to or help prevent the conceptual isolation that Edouard U. talks about?

Week 4 - Olia Lialina - STILL THERE Ruins and Templates of Geocities from Lost and Living (in) Archives
The workarounds that were used in the 90s on geocities especially in lieu of an expansive HTML standard are quite clever. I had previously heard that using HTML tables to lay out a page was common in the past, but I was unaware that they used clear GIFs to help position elements. It's amazing how much of geocities they were able to archive, but I'm glad that now we have things like the internet archive, which thoroughly archives pages alongside their linked resources so we can get snapshots throughout time.

Discussion Questions:
1. Could something like Geocities exist with the same popularity today, or was it a product of its time?
2. Would you have made a homepage on Geocities? What would you have put on it?

Week 5 - Alessandro Ludovico & Florian Cramer - Selections from Publishing from Post-Digital-Print
For me, the ending of Cramer's remarks, that "The book’s references feature extensive web links, some of which I fear may already have expired by the time you are reading these words." perfectly encapsulates a fundamental difference between print media and digital media. Print is extremely stable, at least when compared to digital media. There are cuneiform tablets that we still have from thousands of years ago. On the other hand, there are geocities websites from tens of years ago that are lost to time. Digital media is quite ephermal and that is something that must be considered if an upcoming age of hybrid digital/print media is to arise as Ludovico predicts.

Discussion Questions:
1. Do you prefer print or digital media? Why?
2. Do you think there will be an age of hybrid digital/print media? Are we already in one?

Week 7 - Bojana Coklyat & Shannon Finnegan - Selections from Alt-Text as Poetry Workbook
I had never thought of description of art (previously seen in museums) as something that is colored by our own point-of-view. But, after reading this and looking back it seems obvious now. I think it’s very interesting how with alt-text you can almost add more depth to an art work in a way, by providing a point-of-view or interpretation on it, rather than just the work itself. I also really appreciate the idea that this is just another type of translation between languages, from the visual language to the written one.

Discussion Questions:
1. Should AI supplement the internet by writing alt-text when it is unavailable?
2. Have you ever seen alt-text before when browsing the internet?

Week 8 - Ramsey Nasser - "A Personal Computer for Children of All Cultures" from Decolonising the Digital
It is very interesting to see programming from an outside perspective like this. I’ve occasionally encountered code with identifiers and comments written in another language, and been puzzled. I can’t imagine that being a daily reality for programmers who aren’t familiar with English. This Eurocentrism often even extends into input methods, the most popular input method in mainland China, Pinyin, relies on users being familiar with rather inaccurate “standard” romanizations of Chinese language sounds.

Discussion Questions:
1. Do you speak another language? If so, do you find it harder to use that language digitally than you would for English?
2. Do you think a digital lingua franca is feasible at this point or are we too far gone?
Week 9 - David Reinfurt - Selections from A *New* Program for Graphic Design
As a cognitive science major I found the exploration of psychology and perception really interesting. I've seen a lot of these illusions before in a strictly theoretical neuroscience or psychology context, like the Vase/Faces and the Muller-Lyer lines. I had never really thought about how we can explore these human perceptual quirks in design. Also, I can't believe that I didn't know that the FedEx logo had an arrow until this reading, that's really clever design.

Discussion Questions:
1. Can you think any other examples of logos that "exploit" these principles of perception in a similar way to the examples from Paul Rand?
2. How can the whole being different from the sum of the parts be seen in web design?

Week 10 - Ben Duvall - Selections from New Modernism(s)
The "Clash of Civilizations" chess board is what really helped me understand the argument that Duvall is making. Even with a cursory knowledge of chess and the interactions of the different pieces I was easily able to understand the "hyperlinked" symbolism behind the choices for the icons of each piece. I also appreciate the breakdown of the concept of signs with the signifier and signified as a sort of math equation, making it very easy to understand the differences between modernism, postmodernism and Duvall's hypermodernism.

Discussion Questions:
1. What is an example of a hyperlinked sign in contemporary web design?
2. Is hypermodernism the last step? Could something else come after?

Week 12 - Dot Tuer - Beyond the New Media Frame The Poetics of Absence in Vera Frenkel’s String Games
I find it really interesting how on the pulse of emerging forms of media Frenkel seems to have been. With both television broadcasting and the world wide web, she was able to explore them as new forms of interactive creative expression in their infancy. With both of the works mentioned here exploring loss, I believe these works and their differences speak to inherent differences in the expressiveness of the different mediums (at least at the time). It's an interesting exploration into how one can compensate for limitations of a medium to still show creativity.

Discussion Questions:
1. Are there any emerging mediums right now that are similarly positioned to teleconferencing or the WWW at the time of Frenkel's works?
2. Are we running out of new media as we become more and more digitally connected?

Week 13 - Paul Soulellis - Performing the Feed
Personally, I noticed a huge connection here to the M.T. Anderson novel "Feed". Beyond just the name, the continuous stream of content we're getting through feeds has become arguable akin to having a chip surgically attached to our brain like in the book. It's also interesting how forward-thinking this talk from 2017 was. I had to look back and see when it was done, because at times I thought it was calling out the current state of the internet with LLMs. The feed, and automatically generated content has become more relevant than ever.

Discussion Questions:
1. What are some novel examples of the feed we've seen since this talk in 2017?
2. How has the advent of LLMs (see: ChatGPT) caused the "feed" to evolve?

Week 14 - Boris Groys - Art on the Internet
Groys outlines several ways in which the internet has transformed art into a new era defined by massive exposure, documentation, and surveillance. One thing I found particularly interesting is how they highlight how even with the openness that the internet provides it is still largely corporate-owned. I agree with this sentiment that we have almost gone backwards in some sense with art and media in general on the internet. With video we previously had the DVR, which allowed us to record anything we wanted that came through our television set. Now, in the internet age we have a different three-letter acronym, the TEE (Trusted Execution Environment), a chip installed on your device which you own that you cannot read the data on, which provides the backbone for high-security DRM (Digital Rights Management) software that limits what you can do with media that you have paid for (ex: this is what prevents you from taking a screenshot of Netflix on your phone or computer). As Groys says, with the advent of the internet, we are now under an "algorithmic gaze".

Discussion Questions:
1. Where do we see the "algorithmic gaze" in daily life?
2. Is the internet paradise or hell?
